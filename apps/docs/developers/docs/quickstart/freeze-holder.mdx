As the issuer, you can apply controls to your asset that the network enforces.
In this step you use the simplest one: you freeze the holder's account, watch
their transfer fail, then thaw it so it works again. You can freeze accounts
because step 3 made your wallet the asset's freeze authority.

## Freeze and thaw the holder

Save this as `freeze-holder.ts` next to `client.ts` and run
`npx tsx freeze-holder.ts`. It reads the asset from `asset.json` and the holder
from `holder.json`.

<Freeze />

What the code does:

- `findAssociatedTokenPda(...)` works out the holder's EXF token account, the
  one step 4 opened.
- `sendBack()` builds a transfer of 10 EXF from the holder back to you, signed
  by the holder. Your wallet pays the fee.

Then, in order:

1. **Freeze.** `freezeAccount(...)`, signed by your wallet as the freeze
   authority, freezes the holder's EXF account.
2. **Try a transfer.** Token-2022 rejects any transfer out of a frozen account
   (error 17, `AccountFrozen`). The RPC node simulates the transaction before
   sending it, so it fails there, never lands, and costs nothing. The script
   prints `Transfer blocked: account is frozen`.
3. **Thaw.** `thawAccount(...)` unfreezes the account, and the same transfer
   goes through. The holder ends with 90 EXF.

Freezing only affects this one account for this one asset: the holder's other
tokens, and everyone else's EXF, keep moving. See
[Freeze account](/developers/docs/tokens/basics/freeze-account) for more.
