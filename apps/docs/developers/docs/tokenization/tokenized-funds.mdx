A tokenized fund issues shares priced at a periodic valuation point, whether
the NAV floats or is held stable. The pricing schedule affects several of the
decisions below.

## The sequence

1. **[Design the share](/developers/docs/tokenization/design)**: whether the
   mint or an offchain register is the register of record, and where the share
   price is published.
2. **[Evidence compliance](/developers/docs/tokenization/compliance)**:
   eligibility for the share class, evidenced without publishing holder data.
3. **[Gate the holders](/developers/docs/tokenization/token-acl)**: restrict who
   may subscribe or hold.
4. **[Integrate custody](/developers/docs/tokenization/custody)**: the
   custodian and transfer agent boundary, including
   [vault architectures](/developers/docs/tokenization/custody/vault-architectures)
   where shares are held in a vault.
5. **[Choose the cash leg](/developers/docs/defi/cash-leg)**: what
   subscriptions are paid in and what redemptions pay out.
6. **[Run the pricing cycle](/developers/docs/tokenization/nav-strikes)**:
   strike a net asset value and process subscriptions and redemptions against
   it.

## Fund operations

A token balance usually equals the share count, with the NAV per share
published by the fund administrator or onchain.

[NAV strikes](/developers/docs/tokenization/nav-strikes) works through the
pricing cycle end to end, using a money market fund as its example. The strike
cadence, liquidity model, redemption timing, and regulatory treatment in that
example are specific to money market funds and may differ for other fund
structures.

[Settlement and finality](/developers/docs/defi/finality) covers which
commitment level a fund's books should record.

See [Tokens](/developers/docs/tokens) for the token programs and the extension
reference.
