## Before you take real money

- **Use a production RPC provider.** Public endpoints are rate-limited and not
  meant for production traffic. See [RPC providers](/developers/docs/rpc/providers).
- **Swap devnet values for mainnet ones.** Set `RPC_URL`,
  `RPC_SUBSCRIPTIONS_URL` and `USDC_MINT` from the table below. If you also
  show a Commerce Kit button somewhere, set `config.network` and
  `config.allowedMints` to match.
- **Create the store's USDC account first.** The payment goes to the store
  wallet's USDC token account. If that account doesn't exist, the transfer
  fails. Opening it costs a small refundable deposit (see rent below).
- **Match payments to orders with a reference.** Give every order a fresh
  reference, attach it to the payment as `payStore` does, and check it, as
  `verifyPayment` does. Never match by amount alone: two customers can pay
  \$20 at the same moment. A payment made with Commerce Kit's
  `PaymentButton` carries no order reference, so the confirm endpoint rejects
  it; build checkout payments with your own code.
- **Record each signature once.** Enforce it in your database with a unique
  constraint, so a reused signature can't confirm a second order.
- **Handle expired and failed transactions.** A transaction carries a recent
  blockhash and expires if it isn't confirmed within about 150 blocks, about
  30 seconds at 200ms slots. Let the customer retry, which builds a new transaction.
- **Choose a confirmation level.** `confirmed` is enough for most orders. Wait
  for `finalized` before releasing high-value goods.
- **Use notifications instead of polling.** Get told when your USDC account
  receives funds. See [Indexing and webhooks](/developers/docs/payments/accept-payments/indexing).
- **Decide who pays network fees.** See fee sponsorship below.

## Devnet vs mainnet values

| Setting | Devnet (testing) | Mainnet (real money) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `RPC_URL` | `https://api.devnet.solana.com` | Your RPC provider's HTTPS URL |
| `RPC_SUBSCRIPTIONS_URL` | `wss://api.devnet.solana.com` | Your RPC provider's WebSocket URL |
| `USDC_MINT` | `4zMMC9srt5Ri5X14GAgXhaHii3GnPAEERYPJgZJDncDU` ([Explorer](https://explorer.solana.com/address/4zMMC9srt5Ri5X14GAgXhaHii3GnPAEERYPJgZJDncDU?cluster=devnet)) | `EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v` ([Explorer](https://explorer.solana.com/address/EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v)) |
| USDC decimals | 6 | 6 |
| Commerce Kit `config.network` | `"devnet"` | `"mainnet"` (the default) |
| Money | Free test USDC from the [Circle faucet](https://faucet.circle.com) | Real USDC |

Both mint addresses are Circle's official USDC. Check the mint in
`verifyPayment`, as the example does: anyone can create a token called "USDC",
but only these mints are the real thing.

## Who pays the network fee

In this tutorial's example the customer's wallet is the fee payer, so the customer
needs a little SOL as well as USDC. In the example's output the fee was 5,000
lamports (0.000005 SOL). If your customers only hold dollars, pay the fee for
them.

<SponsorshipConcept />

To sponsor fees, make your server the transaction's fee payer. The customer
still signs the transfer, and your fee-payer key signs for the fee.
[Kora](/developers/docs/tools/kora) runs that fee payer as a service, with validation
rules, rate limits, and the option to charge the fee back in USDC. The
[fee abstraction guide](/developers/docs/payments/send-payments/payment-processing/fee-abstraction)
shows both the basic pattern and Kora.

<RentConcept />

Rent matters for refunds and payouts more than for payments. Your store's USDC
account already exists, but sending USDC to a customer who has never held it
means opening a USDC account for them, and whoever sends pays that deposit.
