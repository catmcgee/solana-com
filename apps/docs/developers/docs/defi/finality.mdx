<Info>
Solana exposes three commitment levels. A system of record should mark a
transfer settled at `finalized`. On mainnet today a block is finalized once 31
further blocks have been built on top of it, so the finalized head trails the
tip of the chain by at least 32 slots (the block plus the 31 above it), and by
more when slots are skipped. An interface may show a user a result at
`confirmed`, which is reached sooner.
[Transaction confirmation and expiration](/developers/cookbook/transactions/confirmation)
explains this in more detail.
</Info>

## Payment, settlement, and finality

- **Payment execution** moves value from one party to another; see
  [Payments](/developers/docs/payments).
- **Settlement** discharges both sides of an obligation, so the asset and the
  cash both end up where they belong; see
  [choosing the cash leg](/developers/docs/defi/cash-leg).
- **Finality** is the point at which the ledger will not reverse the transfer,
  which is a different question from whether your records call it settled.

## The three commitment levels

| Level       | Means                                                        | Reached after          | Can it be reversed?            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| `processed` | The transaction is in a block produced by the current leader | 1 slot                 | Yes, routinely                 |
| `confirmed` | A supermajority of stake has voted for that block            | Typically 1 to 2 slots | Yes, in principle              |
| `finalized` | The block is rooted and a supermajority has built on it      | 31 further blocks      | Only by a supermajority attack |

### Express these in slots

**Record the slot count, derive the wall-clock time, and state the slot time
you assumed.**
[SIMD-0525](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0525-reduce-slot-times.md)
proposes reducing the target slot time from 400ms to 200ms in four
feature-gated steps. The feature gates active on the cluster are the
authoritative state, and [solana.com/200ms](https://solana.com/200ms) shows the
live slot time on mainnet. In client code, read the slot time from the cluster,
not from an SDK constant such as `DEFAULT_MS_PER_SLOT`.

| Target slot time | Status as of October 2026         | 31 blocks, no skipped slots |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| 400ms            | Mainnet                           | About 12.4s                 |
| 350ms            | First step in SIMD-0525           | About 10.9s                 |
| 300ms            | Second step in SIMD-0525          | About 9.3s                  |
| 250ms            | Third step in SIMD-0525           | About 7.75s                 |
| 200ms            | Final step in SIMD-0525           | About 6.2s                  |

## After Alpenglow

[Alpenglow (SIMD-0326)](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0326-alpenglow.md)
replaces TowerBFT with a new voting protocol. As of October 2026 it is active
on devnet and testnet, and mainnet still runs TowerBFT. The 32-slot rule on
this page applies until Alpenglow activates on mainnet.

Under Alpenglow, validators send votes directly to each other rather than as
transactions. A block is finalized in one of two ways: by a fast-finalization
certificate when 80% of stake votes for it in one round, or by a finalization
certificate after two rounds of votes from 60% of stake. Finality no longer
depends on later blocks being built on top.

|                            | TowerBFT (mainnet as of October 2026)     | Alpenglow (after activation)                                   |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| How finality is reached    | 31 further blocks built on the block      | A finalization certificate for the block                       |
| Time to finality           | 31 further blocks                         | One or two rounds of votes                                     |
| Level for a book of record | `finalized`                               | `finalized`                                                    |
| What to record             | Slot, commitment level, slot time assumed | Slot, commitment level, consensus protocol active at that slot |

## Which level to use

| Use                                               | Level       | Basis                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A system of record, releasing an asset            | `finalized` | Reversal needs a supermajority of stake acting against the protocol |
| User-facing feedback                              | `confirmed` | Reached sooner; the block can still be superseded                   |
| Interface feedback where a reversal is acceptable | `processed` | One leader's view; routinely superseded                             |

A block that is not finalized can be superseded in normal operation: the
transaction did not fail, the network built on a different block. Crediting a
balance at `confirmed`, as opposed to displaying it, can book a credit with no
onchain counterpart. Record a superseded transaction as superseded rather than
deleting it, so the history explains why an entry no longer appears.

### Finality and the register of record

Where the mint is the register of record, a finalized transfer updates that
record and no separate book has to be updated. Whether the entitlement has
moved as a matter of law is the legal-finality question below.

Where the token records an asset held elsewhere, a finalized transfer settles
the onchain leg only. The offchain register is updated in a separate step, so
there is an interval in which the token and the register disagree, and the
second update can fail independently of the first. This holds even where the
trade settled atomically against cash: the two token legs move together, and
the register update sits outside that transaction. See
[token design](/developers/docs/tokenization/design) for the distinction and
[choosing the cash leg](/developers/docs/defi/cash-leg) for what the atomic
transaction does and does not complete.

### Blockhash commitment and the validity window

A transaction is valid only while the blockhash it references is recent: the
blockhash is valid for 150 blocks (about 150 slots when none are skipped), and
the runtime rejects anything older. If you fetch that blockhash at
`finalized`, you start from a block that is already at least 32 slots old, so
roughly a fifth of the 150-block window is gone before you have signed
anything.

**Fetch the blockhash at `confirmed`** so the transaction gets its full window,
and use `finalized` to decide whether the result counts.

## Protocol finality is not legal finality

**Protocol finality** is the property described above: the conditions under
which the network will not reorganize a block. It is defined by the protocol
and publicly observable.

**Legal finality** is whether a transfer is irrevocable, and reversible only in
defined exceptions, under the law of a particular jurisdiction, typically in
insolvency. It depends on the venue, the governing law, the legal
characterization of the token, and in some jurisdictions on whether the system
is designated under a settlement finality regime.

Regimes may treat settlement finality differently.

## Ordering

Order events by **slot**, not by block time. Block time is a validator-reported
estimate and is not monotonic across the cluster. Slot numbers are the ledger's
own sequence and are unambiguous.

Where two instructions in the same transaction both affect your books, key
your records on the transaction signature together with the instruction index.
A signature alone is not a unique key for a ledger entry.

## For a regulatory submission

The statements below cover Solana's settlement properties. Each can be checked
independently:

1. Three commitment levels exist, with the meanings above.
2. On mainnet as of October 2026, finality is reached once 31 further blocks
   have been built on the block, so the finalized head trails the tip by at
   least 32 slots. Slot time is a protocol parameter (400ms on mainnet as of
   October 2026); state the slot time assumed alongside any wall-clock figure.
3. Finality rests on economic assumptions about the distribution of stake, and
   is probabilistic in that sense.
4. Legal finality is a separate question determined by jurisdiction, and the
   protocol makes no claim about it.
5. Finality depends on the consensus protocol active at the relevant slots:
   TowerBFT on mainnet as of October 2026, or Alpenglow from its mainnet
   activation slot.

For network liveness history, validator counts, and concentration measures,
take current values from [status.solana.com](https://status.solana.com),
[validators.app](https://www.validators.app), and
[solana.com/data](https://solana.com/data) rather than from documentation.
